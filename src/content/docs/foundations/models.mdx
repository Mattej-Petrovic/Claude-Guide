Anthropic ships Claude in three sizes: Opus, Sonnet, and Haiku. Opus is the most capable, Haiku is the fastest and cheapest, Sonnet is the middle that most people end up using for most things. Each generation gets a version number — the current generation is Claude 4, with three live models inside it: Opus 4.8, Sonnet 4.6, and Haiku 4.5.

If you only remember one thing: **Sonnet 4.6 is the default for most work.** It's what most practitioners run by default, including inside Claude Code. Reach for Opus when the job is genuinely hard or genuinely long-horizon. Reach for Haiku when speed and cost matter more than the last 5% of quality.

## What each model is actually good at

**Opus 4.8** — released May 2026. The most capable model Anthropic ships generally. Built for long-horizon agentic coding, deep reasoning, and tasks where a model needs to stay coherent for hours. The step-change over Opus 4.7 is in long-horizon agentic coding specifically: it handles long context better, compacts less often, and recovers more cleanly when it does, so it stays on task across long autonomous runs instead of drifting. It's also less likely to skip a tool call the task needed, and it's the strongest computer-use and browser-agent model in the family. Use it when you've tried Sonnet and Sonnet wasn't enough, not as a default.

**Sonnet 4.6** — released February 2026. The workhorse. The pattern Anthropic has held across generations is that each new Sonnet matches or beats the previous generation's Opus, and 4.6 is the clearest case of this so far: it handles 90%+ of what people previously reached for Opus to do, at a fifth of the price. It also got a meaningful jump in computer use (browsers, forms, software navigation), which matters if you're using Claude Code, Chrome, or Cowork in agentic modes.

**Haiku 4.5** — released October 2025. The fast-and-cheap tier, but with real intelligence. The reason Haiku matters more than it used to is that 4.5 is the first Haiku that's genuinely useful for non-trivial reasoning, not just classification and extraction. Good for high-volume work, real-time apps, and as a sub-agent inside more complex workflows where Opus or Sonnet is orchestrating.

## Extended thinking and adaptive thinking

Two related but different features:

**Extended thinking** — the model produces visible reasoning steps before answering. Available on Sonnet 4.6 and Haiku 4.5. Not available on Opus 4.8 (Opus uses adaptive thinking instead). Useful when you want to see the model's chain of reasoning, or when a problem benefits from explicit step-by-step deliberation.

**Adaptive thinking** — the model decides on its own whether a turn needs reasoning and how much. Available on Opus 4.8 and Sonnet 4.6. Not on Haiku. In practice this is what you want for most agentic work: you don't tune the depth turn by turn, the model handles it. You still set the ceiling with the **effort** control — low, medium, high, or xhigh — which tells the model how hard to work before it answers. On Opus 4.8 effort defaults to high. In the Claude apps (claude.ai and desktop) this is no longer buried in the API: there's a thinking toggle and an effort selector right next to the model picker, so you can turn reasoning on and dial the depth without leaving the chat.

So: Sonnet 4.6 has both, Opus 4.8 has adaptive only, Haiku 4.5 has extended only. This is one of the more confusing parts of the lineup and worth re-reading once.

## Choosing an effort level

Adaptive thinking decides *whether* the model thinks; **effort** sets *how hard* it works before it answers. It's a broader lever than thinking — it shapes the whole response: how many tool calls the model makes, how much it explores, how much it explains. There are five levels, lowest to highest spend: **low, medium, high, xhigh, max.** The default everywhere is `high`.

Two things to know before the per-level guidance: effort is a control on Opus and Sonnet but **not Haiku** — Haiku has one speed and you take it as-is — and **`xhigh` is Opus-only.** Sonnet tops out at `max`.

What each level is for:

- **low** — fewest tokens, fastest, cheapest. For scoped, well-defined work: quick lookups, classification, high-volume jobs, and subagents doing one clear thing. Pair it with an explicit checklist if the task has multiple parts, or the model may scope down to only what was literally asked.
- **medium** — the balanced middle. Solid results while cutting cost. The right everyday default for Sonnet on agentic and tool-heavy work.
- **high** — the default. Complex reasoning and difficult coding where quality matters more than speed or cost. On Opus this is the floor for anything intelligence-sensitive, and often the sweet spot.
- **xhigh** *(Opus only)* — extended exploration for long-horizon coding and agentic runs: repeated tool calls, deep search, tasks that run for tens of minutes. The recommended *starting* point for serious coding on Opus. Expect meaningfully more tokens than `high`.
- **max** — no ceiling on spend. Reserve it for genuinely frontier problems. On most work it adds cost for small gains, and on structured or simpler tasks it can tip into overthinking.

By model:

- **Opus 4.8** — start at **xhigh** for coding and agentic work; keep **high** as the minimum for other hard reasoning. Step down to **medium** when cost matters, **low** for short scoped tasks and subagents. Save **max** for the rare problem where your own testing shows `xhigh` leaving something on the table. If Opus feels shallow, raise effort before you reach for a longer prompt.
- **Sonnet 4.6** — **medium** is the everyday recommendation: the best speed/cost/quality balance for most apps. Drop to **low** for chat and high-volume or latency-sensitive work; go to **high** when a task genuinely needs the depth, and **max** for the rare hardest cases. No `xhigh` — that's an Opus lever.
- **Haiku 4.5** — no effort control. It's the fast tier; run it as-is and move the job to Sonnet or Opus when it needs tuning.

In the Claude apps, effort is a selector next to the model picker, so you can move between these levels per conversation without touching the API. The same rule of thumb holds everywhere: start one level higher than you think you need, then come down once you've seen the model handle the task.

## Mythos Preview

A fourth model exists but isn't generally available. Claude Mythos Preview is a research preview released in April 2026, focused on defensive cybersecurity. It's distributed through Project Glasswing: invitation-only, no self-serve access, used by partners working on critical infrastructure security. You won't run into it in normal work. It's mentioned here only so the lineup makes sense when you see it referenced elsewhere.

## How to actually pick

Most of the official guidance frames this as a tradeoff between capability, speed, and cost. In practice, the decision is usually simpler than that:

- Default to **Sonnet 4.6**. Run it until it fails you on something specific.
- If it's failing on reasoning depth or long-horizon coherence, move that workflow to **Opus 4.8**. Don't move everything. Just the part that needs it.
- Before you switch models, try turning the **effort** control up. A lot of "Sonnet wasn't deep enough" cases are really "Sonnet on default effort wasn't deep enough" — raising effort (or toggling thinking on in the apps) is cheaper than reaching for Opus and often closes the gap.
- If you're hitting volume or latency walls and the task is well-defined, move that workflow to **Haiku 4.5**. Test it; modern Haiku is more capable than its reputation.
- Don't pick based on price alone. Sonnet at $3/$15 per million tokens is cheap enough that token cost is rarely the right thing to optimize first.

The common mistake is sticking with Opus by default because "more capable = better." For most tasks, Opus is overkill, slower, and more expensive without producing materially better output. Use it where it earns its place.

## What's next

Each generation has been roughly six to nine months apart. Claude 5 is expected sometime in 2026 but not announced as of this writing. When it ships, the pattern to watch is whether Sonnet 5 matches or exceeds Opus 4.8: that's been the through-line across generations and it's the most reliable signal of how to move workflows.
