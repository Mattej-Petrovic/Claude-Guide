Anthropic ships Claude in three sizes: Opus, Sonnet, and Haiku. Opus is the most capable, Haiku is the fastest and cheapest, Sonnet is the middle that most people end up using for most things. Each generation gets a version number — the current generation is Claude 4, with three live models inside it: Opus 4.7, Sonnet 4.6, and Haiku 4.5.

If you only remember one thing: **Sonnet 4.6 is the default for most work.** It's what most practitioners run by default, including inside Claude Code. Reach for Opus when the job is genuinely hard or genuinely long-horizon. Reach for Haiku when speed and cost matter more than the last 5% of quality.

## What each model is actually good at

**Opus 4.7** — released April 2026. The most capable model Anthropic ships generally. Built for long-horizon agentic coding, deep reasoning, and tasks where a model needs to stay coherent for hours. The step-change over Opus 4.6 is in agentic coding specifically: running long autonomous tasks without losing the thread. Use it when you've tried Sonnet and Sonnet wasn't enough, not as a default.

**Sonnet 4.6** — released February 2026. The workhorse. The pattern Anthropic has held across generations is that each new Sonnet matches or beats the previous generation's Opus, and 4.6 is the clearest case of this so far: it handles 90%+ of what people previously reached for Opus to do, at a fifth of the price. It also got a meaningful jump in computer use (browsers, forms, software navigation), which matters if you're using Claude Code, Chrome, or Cowork in agentic modes.

**Haiku 4.5** — released October 2025. The fast-and-cheap tier, but with real intelligence. The reason Haiku matters more than it used to is that 4.5 is the first Haiku that's genuinely useful for non-trivial reasoning, not just classification and extraction. Good for high-volume work, real-time apps, and as a sub-agent inside more complex workflows where Opus or Sonnet is orchestrating.

## Extended thinking and adaptive thinking

Two related but different features:

**Extended thinking** — the model produces visible reasoning steps before answering. Available on Sonnet 4.6 and Haiku 4.5. Not available on Opus 4.7 (Opus uses adaptive thinking instead). Useful when you want to see the model's chain of reasoning, or when a problem benefits from explicit step-by-step deliberation.

**Adaptive thinking** — the model decides on its own how much reasoning a problem needs. Available on Opus 4.7 and Sonnet 4.6. Not on Haiku. This replaced the old manual "effort" parameter from Opus 4.5. In practice, adaptive thinking is what you want for most agentic work: you don't have to tune the depth, the model handles it.

So: Sonnet 4.6 has both, Opus 4.7 has adaptive only, Haiku 4.5 has extended only. This is one of the more confusing parts of the lineup and worth re-reading once.

## Mythos Preview

A fourth model exists but isn't generally available. Claude Mythos Preview is a research preview released in April 2026, focused on defensive cybersecurity. It's distributed through Project Glasswing: invitation-only, no self-serve access, used by partners working on critical infrastructure security. You won't run into it in normal work. It's mentioned here only so the lineup makes sense when you see it referenced elsewhere.

## How to actually pick

Most of the official guidance frames this as a tradeoff between capability, speed, and cost. In practice, the decision is usually simpler than that:

- Default to **Sonnet 4.6**. Run it until it fails you on something specific.
- If it's failing on reasoning depth or long-horizon coherence, move that workflow to **Opus 4.7**. Don't move everything. Just the part that needs it.
- If you're hitting volume or latency walls and the task is well-defined, move that workflow to **Haiku 4.5**. Test it; modern Haiku is more capable than its reputation.
- Don't pick based on price alone. Sonnet at $3/$15 per million tokens is cheap enough that token cost is rarely the right thing to optimize first.

The common mistake is sticking with Opus by default because "more capable = better." For most tasks, Opus is overkill, slower, and more expensive without producing materially better output. Use it where it earns its place.

## What's next

Each generation has been roughly six to nine months apart. Claude 5 is expected sometime in 2026 but not announced as of this writing. When it ships, the pattern to watch is whether Sonnet 5 matches or exceeds Opus 4.7: that's been the through-line across generations and it's the most reliable signal of how to move workflows.
